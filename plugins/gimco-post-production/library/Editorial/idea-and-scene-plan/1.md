## 1. Build the idea before choosing an effect

For every beat:
1. State what the viewer should understand after it. Name its job: question, contrast, cause, process, scale, evidence, reveal, consequence or invitation.
2. Describe the visual's initial state, its change, and its readable final state. For a process, the input reaches its output; for a contrast, the same object changes state. Title when a title explains the line, diagram when the relationship matters, real evidence for a factual case.
3. Give the dominant element a cue: a reviewed spoken word start (recordings) or an authored reading moment (graphics). Enter, demonstrate, resolve, hold. Fit the action to the available time instead of squeezing it.
4. Choose presenter / split / full takeover because it helps this idea. A personal line can return to the speaker; a multi-step explanation may need the full canvas. Keep face, hair, beard and any burned-in subtitles clear.
5. Choose assets and sound for a reason. A real capture proves a state; an illustrative diagram explains a relationship; label examples as examples.

Carry an idea across scenes: a question becomes a diagram, its input becomes results, one result takes focus, the ending answers the opening. Preserve the relevant object, position or direction across cuts. Use colour to mark a chapter, a state change or a reveal. Repeating a zoom, card swap or whoosh on a schedule does not explain anything new.
